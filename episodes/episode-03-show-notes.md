# Episode 3: From Robot Capability to Value

<p>This week on Physical AI Weekly, Sarah and Daniel explore the gap between robot capability and operating value—from ship inspections and adaptive welding to factory throughput and reusable automation software.</p><h3>In this episode</h3><ul><li><strong>Gecko’s expanded Navy ceiling</strong>: Gecko reports an increase from $71 million to $300 million in inspection purchasing capacity—not a $300 million funded order. Its robotic inspections could improve repair decisions, but claimed maintenance savings remain independently unverified.</li><li><strong>Adaptive welding from established suppliers</strong>: Lincoln Electric’s XpertLINC and ESAB’s ROBBI AI combine sensing, robot motion and welding decisions for variable parts and small batches. The potential is less programming and better adaptation to uneven joints; production quality and customer returns remain unverified.</li><li><strong>Zhaofeng’s factory speed gap</strong>: Onsite reporting puts loading by its wheeled Kuavo robots at 35–40 seconds, versus about 15 seconds for skilled workers. Real production work offers a useful reference point, but the customer’s roughly three-year payback and faster loading target remain projections.</li><li><strong>Schaeffler’s reusable automation push</strong>: A development partnership with Wandelbots aims to extend NOVA across Schaeffler’s manufacturing network, sharing applications, models and technical knowledge. Company-wide deployment is an ambition, and the claimed potential for up to 40% lower automation life-cycle costs has no published calculation.</li></ul><p>The common thread is task-level value: purchasing capacity, process expertise and shared software matter only when they translate into reliable work and repeatable deployment.</p><h3>What we're watching</h3><ul><li><strong>Zhaofeng’s loading target</strong>: Whether the factory reaches 15-second loading within the customer’s stated six-to-twelve-month window—and what that means for its projected payback.</li></ul>
